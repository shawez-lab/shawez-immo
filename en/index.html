How a purchase works in France

The steps are the same for residents and for buyers living abroad. A notaire, a public official, handles the sale from the preliminary contract to completion.

  1. Viewing and offer

    You can view in person or first by video. Your offer is made in writing and passed to the seller with our advice.

  2. Preliminary contract

    Once the offer is accepted, both parties sign a preliminary contract, the compromis de vente. As a private buyer you then have a ten-day period during which you may withdraw without giving a reason.

  3. Financing and searches

    The contract usually allows time to obtain a mortgage if you need one. Meanwhile the notaire gathers the title deeds, planning documents and legal searches.

  4. Completion

    About three months after the preliminary contract, the final deed is signed at the notaire’s office, in person or by power of attorney, and you receive the keys.

What to budget on top of the price

Each listing shows the price including the agency fee. Taxes and notaire’s costs come on top: for an existing property they usually amount to around seven to eight per cent of the price. The notaire gives you the exact figure before you sign.

Every listing also shows the surface area and, when available, the energy rating and estimated yearly energy costs.
